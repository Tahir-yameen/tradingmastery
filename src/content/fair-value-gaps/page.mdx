# Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A Fair Value Gap is an **imbalance in the market**.

It happens when price moves so quickly in one direction that it leaves behind an area where **little to no trading occurred**.

---

## What is a Fair Value Gap?

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is formed using a **3-candle pattern**:

👉 When the middle candle is so strong that it creates a gap between the first and third candle.

---

### Simple Definition:

* Candle 1 → normal
* Candle 2 → strong impulsive move
* Candle 3 → does not fully overlap Candle 1

👉 The gap between Candle 1 and Candle 3 = FVG

![Fair value gap creation](/fvg-gap.svg)

*This imbalance appears when a strong move leaves an unfilled area between candles.*

---

## Why FVG Exists

Markets aim for **efficiency**.

When price moves too fast:

* Orders are left unfilled
* Price becomes imbalanced

👉 The market often returns to "fill" this gap before continuing.

---

## Types of Fair Value Gaps

---

### 1. Bullish FVG

* Forms during upward movement
* Gap below current price

👉 Price may return down to fill the gap, then continue upward

---

### 2. Bearish FVG

* Forms during downward movement
* Gap above current price

👉 Price may return up to fill the gap, then continue downward

---

## The Concept of “Filling the Gap”

One of the most important ideas:

> Price often returns to an FVG before continuing its move.

This is called:

👉 **FVG Fill**

![FVG fill / return to imbalance](/fvg-fill.svg)

*Price often revisits the imbalance zone before continuing in the original direction.*

---

## Why This Happens

Institutions:

* Move price aggressively
* Leave imbalance
* Return to fill orders
* Then continue in original direction

---

## FVG as Entry Zones

Instead of entering late:

👉 Use FVG as a precision entry area

Example:

* Uptrend + bullish FVG
  👉 Wait for price to retrace into FVG
  👉 Look for buy opportunity

---

## FVG + Liquidity + Order Block

This is where things become powerful.

High-probability setup:

![FVG + liquidity + order block](/fvg-confluence.svg)

*The strongest setups often combine imbalance, liquidity sweeps, and institutional zones.*

1. Liquidity is taken
2. Price enters order block
3. FVG is present

👉 This creates strong confluence

---

## Strong vs Weak FVG

### Strong FVG:

* Created by strong impulsive move
* Causes break of structure
* Not immediately filled

---

### Weak FVG:

* Small imbalance
* Quickly filled
* Less reliable

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

* Marking every small gap as FVG
* Using FVG in sideways markets
* Entering without confirmation
* Ignoring overall trend

---

## Pro Tip

The best FVG setups:

* Align with higher timeframe direction
* Occur after liquidity sweep
* Exist near order blocks

---

## Simple Trading Idea

* Uptrend + liquidity sweep + bullish FVG
  👉 Buy setup

* Downtrend + liquidity sweep + bearish FVG
  👉 Sell setup

---

## Reality Check

FVG is not a guarantee.

It is:

* A precision tool
* A timing tool
* A confluence tool
