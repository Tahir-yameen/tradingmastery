# Liquidity

If you remember one concept from Smart Money Trading…

Make it this one:

> The market moves because of liquidity.

---

## What is Liquidity?

Liquidity is simply:

👉 **Orders in the market (buy and sell positions)**

More specifically, it refers to areas where:

* Stop losses are placed
* Pending orders exist
* Traders are likely to enter or exit

These areas contain **money**—and that’s what institutions need.

![Liquidity pools](/liquidity-pools.svg)

*Liquidity pools are the areas where buy and sell orders gather before price reacts.*

---

## Why Liquidity Matters

Institutional traders (banks, hedge funds) trade with large volumes.

They cannot just enter the market anywhere.

👉 They need enough orders on the opposite side to fill their positions.

And where do those orders exist?

👉 Where retail traders place them.

---

## Where is Liquidity Found?

Liquidity usually builds up in predictable places:

---

### 1. Above Highs (Buy-Side Liquidity)

* Retail traders place buy stop orders above highs
* Stop losses for sell positions are also placed here

👉 This creates a pool of liquidity above highs

---

### 2. Below Lows (Sell-Side Liquidity)

* Retail traders place sell stop orders below lows
* Stop losses for buy positions are placed here

👉 This creates liquidity below lows

---

## Equal Highs & Equal Lows

These are strong liquidity zones.

### Equal Highs:

* Multiple highs at the same level
  👉 Indicates liquidity resting above

### Equal Lows:

* Multiple lows at the same level
  👉 Indicates liquidity resting below

---

## Liquidity Grab (Stop Hunt)

This is one of the most important concepts.

### What happens:

1. Price moves toward a liquidity area
2. Breaks it briefly
3. Triggers stop losses
4. Then reverses sharply

👉 This is called a **liquidity grab**

![Liquidity grab concept](/liquidity-grab.svg)

*Price often sweeps a zone, triggers stops, and then reverses sharply.*

---

### Why It Happens

Institutions:

* Trigger retail stop losses
* Use those orders as liquidity
* Enter large positions
* Then move price in the real direction

---

## Example Scenario

* Market is in uptrend
* Price forms equal lows
* Suddenly price drops below those lows
  👉 Retail traders panic and sell

Then:
👉 Price reverses upward strongly

This is a classic **sell-side liquidity grab**

---

## Key Mindset Shift

Retail traders think:

❌ “Breakout means continue”

Smart money thinks:

✅ “Breakout might be a trap to grab liquidity”

---

## Liquidity Before Movement

Important rule:

> Price often takes liquidity BEFORE making the real move

![Liquidity sweep before movement](/liquidity-sweep.svg)

*This is why watching for a sweep before a directional move is so important.*

---

## Types of Liquidity

* External liquidity → Above highs / below lows
* Internal liquidity → Within structure (smaller highs/lows)

---

## How to Use Liquidity in Trading

Instead of entering randomly:

1. Identify liquidity zones
2. Wait for price to sweep liquidity
3. Look for confirmation
4. Enter in the opposite direction

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

* Trading every breakout
* Ignoring liquidity zones
* Placing stop losses in obvious areas
* Entering too early before sweep

---

## Pro Tip

The best trades often happen:

👉 After liquidity is taken

Not before.
