# Risk-Reward Ratio

Risk-Reward Ratio (RR) is one of the most important concepts in trading.

It defines:

> How much you risk vs how much you aim to gain.

---

## What is Risk-Reward?

![Risk vs reward concept](/risk-reward-rr.svg)

It compares:

* Risk = how much you lose if the trade fails
* Reward = how much you gain if the trade succeeds

---

### Example:

* Risk: $10
* Reward: $30

👉 Risk-Reward = 1:3

---

## Why Risk-Reward Matters

You do NOT need to win every trade.

You only need:

> Good risk-reward + decent win rate

---

## Common Risk-Reward Ratios

![Common RR ratios](/risk-reward-ratios.svg)

### 1:1

* Risk = Reward
* Needs very high win rate to be profitable

---

### 1:2 (Recommended)

* Risk $10 → Reward $20
* Balanced approach

---

### 1:3 (Professional Style)

* Risk $10 → Reward $30
* Allows profitability even with lower win rate

---

## Simple Truth

![Profitability with lower win rate](/risk-reward-profit.svg)

Even if you only win:

👉 40% of your trades
You can still be profitable with good RR

---

## Example Breakdown

Let’s say:

* 10 trades
* Risk = $10 each

### Scenario:

* Win 4 trades (1:3 RR) → +$120
* Lose 6 trades → -$60

👉 Net profit = +$60

---

## Why Beginners Fail

Most beginners:

* Take profits too early
* Cut winners short
* Let losses run

👉 This destroys risk-reward structure

---

## Correct Mindset

Professionals think:

❌ “I want to win every trade”

✅ “I want winners to be bigger than losers”

---

## How to Set Risk-Reward Properly

Before entering a trade:

1. Define entry
2. Define stop loss
3. Define take profit

Then check:

👉 Is reward at least 2x risk?

If not:

❌ Skip the trade

---

## Risk-Reward + Strategy

Good trading is not about signals.

It is about:

* Structure
* Entry precision
* RR discipline

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes

Avoid:

* Moving stop loss wider
* Closing winners too early
* Ignoring take profit levels
* Entering trades without RR planning

---

## Pro Tip

Even with a low win rate, you can succeed if:

👉 Risk-Reward is strong and consistent

---

## Golden Rule

> Protect downside, let upside run.
