# Trendlines

If support and resistance are horizontal levels…

Then trendlines are **diagonal levels that show direction**.

They help you understand not just *where* price reacts—but *how it is moving over time*.

---

## What is a Trendline?

A trendline is a line drawn on a chart that connects:

* Higher lows in an uptrend
* Lower highs in a downtrend

It acts as a **dynamic support or resistance**.

![Uptrend line concept](/trendline-uptrend.svg)

*An uptrend line connects higher lows and helps reveal the direction of momentum.*

---

## Types of Trendlines

There are two main types:

---

### 1. Uptrend Line (Bullish Trendline)

An uptrend line is drawn by connecting:

👉 Higher lows (HL)

It shows that buyers are consistently pushing price higher.

### What it tells you:

* Market is bullish
* Dips are being bought
* Momentum is upward

---

### 2. Downtrend Line (Bearish Trendline)

A downtrend line is drawn by connecting:

👉 Lower highs (LH)

It shows that sellers are in control.

![Downtrend line concept](/trendline-downtrend.svg)

*Downtrend lines connect lower highs and help confirm bearish pressure.*

### What it tells you:

* Market is bearish
* Rallies are being sold
* Momentum is downward

---

## Why Trendlines Matter

Trendlines help you:

* Identify market direction
* Find entry points
* Spot reversals early
* Avoid trading against momentum

---

## How to Draw a Trendline (Correct Way)

Follow these steps:

1. Identify clear swing points (highs or lows)
2. Connect at least 2–3 points
3. Extend the line into the future
4. Don’t force the line—let price define it

---

## Trendline as Dynamic Support & Resistance

Unlike horizontal levels:

* Uptrend line = support
* Downtrend line = resistance

Price often reacts at these lines repeatedly.

---

## Break of Trendline

When price breaks a trendline:

👉 It signals a possible change in momentum

### Example:

* Uptrend line breaks downward
  👉 Possible shift to bearish structure

* Downtrend line breaks upward
  👉 Possible shift to bullish structure

---

## Retest Concept

A very important idea:

After a breakout, price often comes back to test the broken trendline.

This is called a:

👉 Retest

![Trendline retest example](/trendline-retest.svg)

*A retest often gives a high-quality opportunity when the broken line is respected again.*

It often gives strong trading opportunities.

---

## Common Mistakes

Avoid these:

* Forcing trendlines to fit the chart
* Using too few touch points
* Drawing on low timeframes only
* Ignoring market structure

---

## Pro Tip

Trendlines are strongest when:

* Combined with market structure
* Confirmed on higher timeframes
* Used with support/resistance zones

---

## Simple Trading Logic

* In an uptrend → Buy near trendline support
* In a downtrend → Sell near trendline resistance
